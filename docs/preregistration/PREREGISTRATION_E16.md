# Pre-registration: relation mismatch (E16) and prior-evidence compatibility

Written and committed **before the E16 generator existed and before any E16 data
were produced**. The repository state the paper had reached at that moment is
tagged `v0.2-pre-mismatch`, so that anything the mismatch work changes can be
separated from the results it must not be tuned against.

## Why this experiment exists

The Kubernetes campaign (§2.10) produced a prior that was correct as a call
graph and harmful as a prior for observational causal dependence: the dependence
in that deployment is dominated by a shared workload driver the call graph does
not represent, and admissibility under the call graph was no more likely for a
strongly dependent pair (32.4%) than for an arbitrary one (31.5%).

Neither prior-quality axis the paper measures can express that defect. Both
corruption (E2, E13) and coarseness (E7, E8) perturb edges *within* the relation
that the prior and the data share. A prior that describes a *different relation*
is a third failure mode, and our synthetic protocol cannot currently produce one.
E16 adds it.

## The generator

Observed variables follow the existing structural equations with one additional
term: a scalar exogenous driver `L_t`, unobserved, shared by all variables.

    X_i(t) = f_i(Pa_i(t)) + rho_i * X_i(t-1) + alpha_i * L_t + eps_i(t)

`L_t` is a slow AR(1) process standardised to unit variance, chosen to resemble
the load generator of the testbed rather than white noise. The mismatch level
`m` fixes the share of each variable's *exogenous* variance contributed by the
driver,

    alpha_i = noise_scale_i * sqrt(m / (1 - m)),

so that m = 0 reproduces the present benchmark exactly and m -> 1 removes the
idiosyncratic noise entirely. We sweep m in {0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 0.95};
m = 1 is excluded because it makes every variable a deterministic function of
the driver and its parents, which is a different degeneracy.

The structural prior is unchanged: the admissible superset of the true DAG over
the observed variables. It is therefore *correct about the mechanism* at every
value of m, and says nothing about the driver — exactly the deployment situation.
The recovery target is also unchanged: the true DAG over the observed variables.

Two arms:

- **unobserved driver** (the Kubernetes situation, regime K1): the driver is not
  a column of X.
- **observed driver** (regime K3): the driver is appended as an extra variable
  whose prior row and column are unconstrained, which is how an undeclared
  component enters a real manifest. Metrics are reported both over the full
  matrix and restricted to the observed-variable block, so the two arms are
  comparable.

## The statistic

`spcrl/priors/compatibility.py`, committed with this document, defines

    C = max(0, 2 * AUC - 1)

where AUC is the probability that a randomly chosen admissible variable pair has
higher observed dependence than a randomly chosen inadmissible pair. C needs no
ground truth and no threshold, is invariant to monotone rescaling of the
dependence measure, and is therefore computable on a real deployment. It is not
a measure of prior correctness: it ignores direction, and a prior can be correct
about the mechanism and still score zero.

Its behaviour on three constructed cases, run before any E16 data existed:
dependence generated by the prior's own relation gives C = 0.851; the same
system with a shared driver dominating gives C = 0.192; a random prior on the
original data gives C = 0.000.

## Hypotheses

- **H1.** C decreases monotonically in m.
- **H2.** The prior advantage over the prior-blind control,
  `Δ(m) = AUPRC(soft) − AUPRC(none)`, decreases in m and crosses zero at some
  m* < 1. The hard mask crosses no later than the soft prior.
- **H3.** C predicts Δ *across* conditions, not only within this sweep: pooling
  the E16 cells with the existing corruption (E2/E13) and coarseness (E7/E8)
  cells, the Spearman correlation between C and Δ is at least 0.7. This is the
  hypothesis that makes C useful rather than merely descriptive, and the one we
  expect to be hardest to satisfy, because corruption and coarseness damage the
  prior in ways C is deliberately blind to.
- **H4.** The reliability gate of §2.4, which learns a scalar from (X, S), does
  **not** recover the prior advantage as m grows: it was trained under corrupted
  priors, not mismatched ones, and nothing in its objective distinguishes an
  uninformative prior from a hard problem.
- **H5 (observed driver).** With the driver observed and unconstrained, the
  prior advantage on the observed-variable block is larger than in the
  unobserved arm at the same m.

## What is explicitly not a test

Computing C on the already-collected Kubernetes telemetry is a **consistency
check, not evidence**: the coverage numbers from that campaign are what motivated
the definition of C, so C cannot be validated on them. Whatever value it takes
there will be reported as a descriptive statistic of a dataset the statistic was
designed after.

## Decision rule, fixed in advance

If H1 and H2 hold, E16 becomes a results section characterising a third failure
mode, and C is reported as a diagnostic.

Only if **H3 also holds** will we build a compatibility-aware weighting
(`S_effective = q * S` with q estimated from data) and claim it as a method. If
H3 fails, we report the failure mode and the fact that we could not construct a
statistic that predicts prior usefulness across defect types, and we do not
propose a mechanism that works on one axis only.

If H2 fails — the prior keeps its advantage as m grows — then the Kubernetes
result is not explained by relation mismatch, our stated explanation of §2.10 is
wrong, and that must be said in the paper.

We will not change m's definition, the sweep, C, or these hypotheses after
seeing the results. Any change will be recorded as an amendment below, with its
date and reason.

## Amendments

**Amendment 1 (2026-09-17): the driver is calibrated against total variance, not
noise variance, and its loadings may be heterogeneous.**

As pre-registered, `alpha_i = noise_scale_i * sqrt(m / (1 - m))` sizes the driver
as a share of each variable's *exogenous noise*. Implementing it showed that this
does not produce the intended regime: the parent terms in these mechanisms carry
weights of 0.5-2.0 against noise scales of 0.4-1.0, so the structural signal
stays dominant even at m = 0.95 and the compatibility statistic falls only from
0.242 to 0.116 - nowhere near the deployment value. A quantity called "the share
of dependence due to the driver" that leaves the dependence structure mostly
intact is simply mis-specified.

`attach_driver` therefore simulates each system once with the driver off,
measures each variable's standard deviation `v_i`, and sets
`alpha_i = v_i * sqrt(m / (1 - m)) * u_i`, so that the driver contributes the
intended share of *total* variance. `u_i` is a lognormal multiplier with unit
mean and standard deviation `spread`, which lets components differ in how
strongly they respond to the shared driver, as real components do. With this
parameterisation C falls from 0.242 to 0.000 (spread = 0) and to 0.023
(spread = 0.8) across the sweep, the latter close to the 0.02 the deployment
telemetry gives.

The sweep now crosses `spread` as a second factor rather than replacing the
pre-registered homogeneous case: `spread` in {0, 0.8}.

This amendment was made **before any outcome quantity was computed**. At the
time of writing, the only statistic that had been evaluated on the new generator
was C itself - the diagnostic - and no AUPRC, no prior advantage and no model had
been trained on driver data. It therefore cannot have been fitted to Δ(m), and
the hypotheses H1-H5 and the decision rule are unchanged.
